# 04 — Rendering

The renderer is in the **freedom-of-implementation** zone (ADR-007): it may have a different internal structure, a different precision and no fixed limits, as long as the visual result is DOOM's.
In **vanilla mode** (320×200, 4:3, precision fixes off), the frame must be **pixel-identical** to Chocolate Doom's (G2). That is what gives us an objective test for the renderer.

## 1. Frame pipeline

```
Camera (interpolated)           ← PlayerView + alpha (§7)
  │
  ├─ 1. setup: angle, position, viewz, fixed colormap (invulnerability, light amplification visor), extralight
  ├─ 2. front-to-back BSP → visible segs
  │      ├─ solid walls and upper/lower parts → columns (ColumnDrawer)
  │      ├─ floors/ceilings → collection of visplanes by (height, flat, light) + open vertical bands
  │      └─ "masked" middle textures and silhouettes → drawsegs for later
  ├─ 3. projection of the sprites of the visited sectors → vissprites
  ├─ 4. visplanes → horizontal spans (SpanDrawer); sky as columns (§5)
  ├─ 5. vissprites sorted back to front + masked midtextures, clipped by drawsegs
  ├─ 6. psprites (weapon and flash) on top
  └─ 7. 2D layer: status bar/HUD, messages, automap, menu, wipe (§6)
        → FrameBuffer (byte[] of indices) + current palette → VideoSink
```

The internal structure may be different (for example, the "single BSP walk" that Carmack himself suggests in the 1997 README), as long as vanilla mode remains pixel-identical.

## 2. Resolution and projection parameters

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| `H` | Internal height of the framebuffer |
| `A` | Aspect ratio of the output area (width/height): 4/3, 16/9, 16/10, 64/27 (21:9), 43/18 (3440×1440)… |
| `W` | Internal width: `round(H × 1.2 × A)` with aspect correction; `round(H × A)` in "square pixels" |
| `W43` | Equivalent 4:3 width: `H × 1.2 × 4/3 = 1.6 H` (or `H × 4/3` in square pixels) |
| `projection` | `W43 / 2` in fixed-point. It is the same for X and Y, as in the original |
| `centerX`, `centerY` | `W/2`, `viewHeight/2` |

- **Automatic mode (default):** `H = outputHeight / 1.2` and `W = outputWidth`. Horizontally, each internal pixel becomes exactly one screen pixel; the presentation only stretches vertically. Example: a 2560×1080 window → internal 2560×900.
- **Render scale** (0.25–2.0): multiplies `H` and `W`. Serves as a performance valve or as supersampling.
- **Vanilla mode:** forces 320×200, 4:3 and turns off the precision fixes.
- The view tables (`xToViewAngle[W]`, `viewAngleToX`, `distScale[W]`, `ySlope[H]`) are **generated by the renderer** for each `W×H`. The horizontal FOV goes beyond 90° when `A > 4/3`: it is the tables that absorb this (Hor+, ADR-010).
- The screen size (`screenblocks` 3–11) defines the view window inside the framebuffer, with the original's beveled border and background *flat*. The values 10 and 11 (no border; 11 without the status bar) are the usual ones in widescreen.

## 3. Precision at high resolution

DOOM's renderer was made for 320 columns. At high resolutions, some 32-bit calculations show artifacts.
With `precisionFixes = true` (the default outside vanilla mode), the remake uses `long` calculation or higher-resolution tables at the known points:

| Artifact | Fix |
|----------|-----|
| "Wall wiggle" (walls undulating while turning) | Wall scale computed with 64 bits |
| Error on very long walls | Vertex angles with full precision (without going through the coarse `tantoangle` table) |
| Scale overflow of sprites and very close walls | Limits and calculations in `long` |
| Steps on distant floors | Span `xStep`/`yStep` with more precision |

Rule: these versions have **different names** from the functions that the simulation uses (the simulation's `PointToAngle` stays untouched in `math`, ADR-008).

## 4. Textures, flats, lighting

- **Composed textures** (TEXTURE1/TEXTURE2 + PNAMES + patches): composed at load time into contiguous columns, with a per-texture cache.
- **Non-power-of-2 height:** the original masks the vertical coordinate with `& 127`, which produces the "tutti-frutti" on textures whose height differs from 128 or from smaller powers of 2. In vanilla mode, reproduce the *wrap* at 128. Outside it, `correctTextureWrap = true` uses the modulo by the real height.
- **Medusa effect** (multi-patch masked midtextures) and **visplane overflow**: the original fails or corrupts the image. The remake does **not** reproduce this. It is a documented `DIVERGE`.
- **Lighting:** sector light level `>> 4` + `extralight` (weapon flash); fake contrast (axis-aligned walls ±1 level); walls and sprites indexed by **scale** (`scalelight`), floors by **distance** (`zlight`); 32 colormaps + invulnerability.
  **Attention:** the light index by scale depends on the width of 320. At high resolution, the scale is **normalized by `W43/320`** before indexing, so that light falls off with distance exactly as in the original.
- **Fuzz (Spectre and invisibility):** the original's 50-entry offset table. At high resolution, the offset is applied in blocks of `H/200` rows and columns (option `fuzzStyle = scaled | pixel`), preserving the original "grain".
- **Palettes:** 14 PLAYPAL palettes (normal, 8 damage, 4 item, radiation suit), chosen from the player state as in the original.

## 5. Sky

- Sky columns mapped by the view angle (the texture repeats 4 times over 360°). In widescreen, more angles are simply sampled.
- Texture height and middle as in the original. Fixed vertical scale, independent of the FOV.
- Sky texture by episode or by map range according to the `GameMode` (including DOOM II's rule that switches the sky by map range).

## 6. 2D layer and widescreen

- **Virtual 320×200 canvas**, drawn with scale `s = H/200` (may be fractional; *nearest* sampling) and centered in X. Origin: `xOffset = (W − 320·s)/2`.
- **Patches wider than 320** (widescreen assets of the re-releases): drawn respecting the `leftoffset`, that is, centered on the canvas and spilling over the sides.
- **Empty sides** (pillarbox) in menus, intermission and finale: black by default; optionally the game's border *flat*.
- **Status bar:** 320×32 STBAR centered, with the sides filled by (1) a widescreen STBAR if the patch is wider, or (2) the border *flat* tiled. The numbers and faces use the same positions as the original, relative to the bar.
- **Full-screen HUD** (`screenblocks = 11`): the original shows only messages. The remake offers an optional **minimal HUD** (health, armor, ammo, keycards), made with the WAD's own graphics and alignable to the corners or to the 4:3 center. It is a new presentation feature; it is off in vanilla mode.
- **Weapon (psprites):** original coordinates (320×200 base) × `s`, centered in X. It does not stretch with widescreen.
- **Automap:** drawn at native `W×H`, with line thickness proportional to `s`. Zoom and *pan* are interpolated.
- **Wipe (melt):** 2-pixel strips of the 320 space, scaled (strip width = `2·s`). In widescreen the number of strips grows. Initial delays come from the "misc" RNG, as in the original.

## 7. Uncapped FPS and interpolation

The renderer receives `alpha ∈ [0, 1]`: the fraction between the previous tic and the current one.

| What is interpolated | Source |
|----------------------|--------|
| Position and angle of actors | `prevX/Y/Z/Angle` → current |
| Camera (position, `viewz`, *bob*) | Local player, as above |
| Heights of moving sector floors and ceilings | `prevFloorHeight/prevCeilingHeight` recorded at the start of the tic |
| Weapon (`sx`, `sy` of the psprites) | Previous and current values |
| Texture *scroll* (special line 48) | Previous and current offset |
| Automap | Position, zoom and markers |

- **Angles** interpolate through the signed `int` difference (`prev + (int) ((long) (cur − prev) · alphaFixed >> 16)`), which always gives the shortest path.
- **No interpolation** on the spawn tic, on teleport, at level start and when loading a save. The simulation calls `actor.snapInterpolation()` at those points. It is a write only to presentation fields, allowed by ADR-011.
- **Local player's per-frame turn:** the mouse input accumulated since the last tic is added to the camera angle **immediately** (without waiting for the next tic). On the next tic, that accumulation becomes the `TicCmd`'s `angleTurn` (quantized to 16 bits, or to 8 bits when recording a demo) and the quantization **residue** is left for the next tic. That way, the camera never strays from the simulation's real angle by more than one tic of input.
- Turned off in `-timedemo`, in vanilla mode and with the option `uncappedFramerate = false`.
- **Pacing:** vsync (default) or limiter (`maxFps`: 0 = unlimited), through a hybrid wait (`parkNanos` + a short *spin* at the end).

## 8. Presentation (OpenGL 3.3 core)

- Textures: `index` (`GL_R8`, `W×H`, updated every frame via `glTexSubImage2D` from a direct `ByteBuffer`) and `palette` (`GL_RGBA8`, 256×1, updated only when the palette or the gamma change).
- Shader: `color = texelFetch(palette, ivec2(texelFetch(index, coord).r * 255, 0))`. Scaling is done on the full-screen *quad*.
- **Gamma:** the original's 5 gamma curves applied to the palette on the CPU before upload. Optionally, continuous gamma in the shader.
- **Scaling:** `fit` (correct aspect, with bars), `integer` (integer multiples), `stretch`. `nearest` or `sharp-bilinear` filtering (bilinear only at the pixel edges).
- **HiDPI:** uses the size of the GLFW *framebuffer* (not that of the window) to compute the output.
- **Screenshot:** PNG from the indexed framebuffer + palette (in the core, without GL).
- **Framebuffer layout:** it can be by rows or **transposed** (by columns). Transposed keeps columns (walls, sprites, sky) sequential in memory, and the transposition comes for free in the shader. The decision comes out of the milestone M6 benchmark ([10](10-roadmap.md)). The renderer code accesses the framebuffer through a `FrameLayout` that hides this.

## 9. Performance

**Targets** (2020+ CPU, such as Apple M1 or Ryzen 5000; `-timedemo` demo on typical DOOM II maps):

| Internal resolution | Target |
|---------------------|--------|
| 320×200 | > 2000 fps (sanity) |
| 2560×900 (2560×1080 at 21:9, automatic) | ≥ 200 fps average, p1 ≥ 144 |
| 3840×1800 (automatic 4K) | ≥ 90 fps average |

**Strategies**, in order:
1. Lean column and span loops: local arrays, no virtual calls in the inner loop, colormap as a flat `byte[]`. Verify with JMH that the JIT eliminates the bounds checks.
2. Transposed framebuffer layout (§8), if the benchmark confirms the gain.
3. Render scale < 1 as a valve for weaker machines.
4. **Parallelism by vertical strips** (post-v1): `N` threads, each with its own range of columns and its own clipping structures, walking the BSP with strip clipping. The traversal is repeated, but the drawing is split. Sprite sorting and the 2D layer remain serialized.
